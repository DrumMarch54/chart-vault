## Introduction to numerology

### A DEFINITION OF NUMEROLOGY

Numerology is an ancient esoteric practice with roots dating back to various ancient cultures, involving the study of numerical values and their influence on human life and events. The essence of numerology is the belief that numbers have symbolic and spiritual significance and can reveal deeper insights into our existence.

There are many different branches of esoteric sciences about the impact and effect of numbers, which we today call ”numerology,” and they have gone under many different names in their respective cultures and time periods.

The word numerology is created from the Latin word ”numerus” (number) and the Greek word “logia”, from logos, (meaning the study of, knowledge, thought, or expression) and in this way a very broad description of what the field covers.

Numerology, understood as a broad collective term for everything that pertains to the symbolic and spiritual significance of numbers, also contains many different methods and practices, as you will see in this book.

If we are to explain what the essence of numerology is, we can say that it is about decoding the hidden energetic connections in life. The language of numerology is numbers, and in this context, numbers are expressions of energy and vibration.

Numerological methods involve everything from reading codes in sacred works, to decoding the energies of days, months, and years, to reading the individual energies of a person based on names and birth dates, as we see in all the major numerology systems. Numerology is also the attempt to decode the rhythms of the universe over long periods, as we see in the Mayan culture and in the ancient Indian texts, the Vedas.

To understand numerology as a field, and our own place in history,

it is important to consider all the different parts of numerology’s development. In the book, we therefore cover a rich variety of topics within numerology, including the following:

#### The Origins of Numerology

Based on historical sources, we can establish that numerology exists in various ancient civilizations such as India, the Maya culture, Egypt, Mesopotamia, Sumer, and ancient Greece. There are writings and calculations within numerology from these ancient cultures. Numerology is assumed to be many thousands of years old. Even in ancient scriptures like the Vedas, which are more than 5000 years old, one finds numerological calculations.

Looking back at ancient civilizations’ most developed civilizations, one sees a pattern – namely, that they all used numerology formed by a deep understanding of the numbers’ significance as universal archetypes and their relation to sacred geometry.

It is also clear that no culture stood alone but was influenced by other cultures. For example, the Greeks traveled to Egypt, where they received sacred knowledge from Egyptian priests. Pythagoras spent 20 years in Egypt, and he also had long wanderings in Chaldea (Mesopotamia) and visited Indian sages, who also practiced numerology. A common denominator for these cultures is numerical calculations within architecture, patterns, and aesthetics, the significance of numbers in creation, as well as cycles and calendar systems.

#### Cuneiform Script

Chaldean numerology was originally based on cuneiform, a wedge-shaped script.

There are different conventions for transliterating cuneiform into the Latin alphabet, as cuneiform was used by various civilizations like the Sumerians, Akkadians, Babylonians, and Assyrians over many thousands of years. Therefore, there are several variations of cuneiform. Cuneiform can be translated into the Latin alphabet using transliteration. Transliteration is the process of rewriting characters from one script to another, preserving the sound of the characters but writing them with letters from the new script system.

For example, a cuneiform character representing the sound ”a” can be transliterated to ”a” in the Latin alphabet. A cuneiform character representing the sound ”ka” is transliterated to ”ka,” and so on. It’s important to note that transliteration is not a translation in the sense that the Latin alphabet carries the same meaning as the cuneiform characters. Transliteration is just a method of representing the phonetic sound of cuneiform characters in another script system. Transliteration of cuneiform can be a challenging task, especially because some cuneiform characters represent more than a single sound. It requires a good understanding of cuneiform and the linguistic sounds it represents to perform an accurate transliteration.

#### Translation from Cuneiform to Latin Letters

The timeline for the translation of cuneiform into the Latin alphabet and the assignment of numerical values is not clearly documented. In cuneiform’s numerical system, each cuneiform character had its own numerical value based on its phonetics.

When cuneiform was translated into the Latin alphabet, these were again linked with numerical values based on their phonetics. The earliest known attempts, according to mainstream research, to translate cuneiform into Latin letters were first performed in the 19th Century and onwards.
