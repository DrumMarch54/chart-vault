### B. TAROT CARDS

Tarot cards are a collection of symbol-rich images, primarily used for predictions, meditation, and psychological insight. Their origins and function have fascinated and perplexed people for centuries and continue to offer deep insights into personal and collective psyche.

#### Origins

The origins of tarot cards are debated, but many believe they date back to the 15th century in Europe, possibly Italy or France. Tarot cards became rich in symbolism and were interpreted as metaphors for the path of life or as spiritual lessons.

However, some believe they are based on a series of Egyptian cards, stretching even further back in history to ancient Egypt.

#### Symbolism

A typical Tarot deck consists of 78 cards, divided into two main categories: Major Arcana (22 cards) and Minor Arcana (56 cards). The Major Arcana cards are the most well-known and symbol-heavy, representing important life lessons and archetypes. The Minor Arcana cards are more like a regular deck of playing cards, divided into four types and numbered from ace to ten, along with four court cards. Major Arcana: Each card, from the Fool (0) to the World (21), symbolizes a station on the individual’s journey towards self-knowledge and realization.

Minor Arcana: These cards represent the more mundane aspects of life and are divided into four types, which are typically swords, goblets, staffs, and pentacles. Each type relates to an aspect of human life.

### C. RESEARCH IN THE SOURCES OF NUMEROLOGY

This part of our historical research involves uncovering all the sources that exist that deal with numerology in one form or another. Both the sources we call ’hidden’, which only exist in a few copies worldwide and must be obtained through collectors and private libraries - as well as the known sources that can be bought as books online or found in libraries.

We look at all the sources available on numerology and analyze them, compare them and extract the essences.

Our research into the sources shows a lot of evidence that all the major numerology systems (Chaldean, Pythagorean and Indian) were originally much closer to each other than they are today and share many of the same origins.

Historically, we can see that there is something fundamental that is common to several systems, but which later splits into several branches, whereby different parts of the original knowledge have been lost. We try to recreate this forgotten knowledge from the sources.

In some numerology systems, the very foundations have been lost and only reminiscences of the original system exist. But the things that still exist can contribute to deeper understanding. An example is the letter-numeral alphabet based on phonetics, sound, vibration and sound (pronunciation), which is only preserved in the Chaldean (and Indian) system, but lost in the Pythagorean system.

The numeroscope in its full version with all the number positions is only preserved in the Chaldean, where the Pythagorean only has a few of the numbers left (the name values and the Life Expression).
