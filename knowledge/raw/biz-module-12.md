#### Inc. ESTABLISHMENT

- When you set up a U.S. corporation (Inc.), you have to deal with two dates:

- 1. The Date of Incorporation This is the most important date numerologically. It must be the super date you have chosen.

It is the date that appears in the state’s official corporate registry (for example, the Delaware Secretary of State database).

This is considered the company’s birth date in all legal and numerological terms.

- 2. The Effective Date The “Effective Date” is when the corporation officially becomes active for practical purposes.

It can be the same as the incorporation date, but accountants or incorporation services sometimes set it a few days or weeks later.

- If they do, you are free to choose the next date that carries the same GV. For example, if you incorporated on March 23, you can set the effective date to April 5 or April 14, as these carry the same vibration.

#### NEW DATE – TWO METHODS

If you already have a single person comp.

- 1. Create a new business entity (Inc. or LLC)

Inform the client that forming a U.S. corporation or LLC involves additional paperwork (articles of incorporation/organization, bylaws/operating agreement, and state filings).

Refer them to an accountant or formation service if needed — professional help typically starts around $200–$300 in the U.S.

- 2. Close the existing sole proprietorship The client must check with their accountant whether it is possible — and advisable — to close their current sole proprietorship if there are still ongoing sales or contracts.

Note: When moving from a sole proprietorship to an LLC or Inc., the business will receive a new EIN (tax ID). This is normal and perfectly fine.
