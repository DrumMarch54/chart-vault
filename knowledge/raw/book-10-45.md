### A Bit of Theory: The Compound Numbers

In numerology, compound numbers are all numbers with two or more digits, i.e., numbers greater than 9. We assume you are already somewhat familiar with the numbers 1-9 and their archetypal meanings. So why are compound numbers interesting?

Compound numbers tell the story of the relationship between two numbers, and thereby between two archetypes.

For example, if we look at the number 14/5, this can also be understood as the relationship between the number 1 and the number 4 and how these two numbers ”interact” with each other.

If, for instance, one person has a base vibration of 1, meaning they were born on the 1st of the month, and another person has a base vibration of 4, their relationship and energy exchange can largely be described by the number 14. This number essentially describes the interaction between the number 1 and the number 4.

But it’s not only in relationships between two people that the connection between their base vibrations can be described by a compound number. Your own base vibration also forms a relationship with, for instance, your month vibration or year vibration. Similarly, your base vibration creates a relationship with other external influences, such as a house number. If you are a 1 living in house number 4, you also form a 14 vibration together with the house’s energy.

Whenever two or more numbers meet, they begin to resonate together, creating new numbers and vibrations.

### What is the little diamond?

In numerological notation, we use a model called the ‘diamond,’ which helps us delve deeply into a number’s vibrations. The diamond shape is part of numerology’s traditional framework, following the same principles as the lower part of the numeroscope, where names are combined to create the life expression. However, in this case, the names in a numeroscope are replaced by the numbers in the compound number being analyzed.

For example, let’s examine the number 14/5 (see the diamond on the left). We place the 1 in the top-left corner of the diamond and the 4 in the top-right corner. Together, they produce the 5, also called the number’s life expression, which is placed at the bottom of the diamond.

From this, we derive what are known as the aura numbers, and finally, the essence number is calculated by combining the aura numbers. In the same way that the solar plexus number in a numeroscope reveals much about a person’s inner emotional life, the essence number here provides insight into the number’s inner feelings. In terms of importance, the life expression (the digit sum) carries the most weight, followed by the outer left number (in this case, the 1), and finally the inner right number (in this case, the 4). Thus, 14/5 is primarily a 5 energy, secondarily a 1 energy, and lastly a 4 energy. As a general rule: The life expression contributes approximately 50% of the energy. The outer number contributes about 30%.
